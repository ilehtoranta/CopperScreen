## How the workload improved

The first integer field was slow because device deadlines repeatedly cut
through a hot Fast RAM loop. The interpreter picked up roughly 129,000
instructions per field at interior PCs while individual traces were warming.
Copper68k now promotes the whole bounded loop, eliminates repeated work
where register and flag dependencies allow it, and resumes generated code at
a verified instruction index after each device deadline. The first integer
field fell from 225.8 million CPU-owner cycles with the prefix-only build to
79.8 and 77.5 million in two resume prototypes.

The accelerated runner uses 256 CPU timing units per PAL board cycle. Fast
RAM and loaded ROM can serve CPU transfers directly. Chip RAM and I/O
transfers retain their motherboard timing. Video and DMA advance on the
canonical board clock. A timed CPU transfer or pending device deadline
returns a compiled slice through the normal boundary.

  68040 JIT ran at 256× with the historical CopperStart ROM and a read-only
  HDF mapped as 'DH0:1:32:0:1023'.
- The first Speed click starts at field 810. Warm measurements take the
  second click at field 1460 after a completed run. The sampler calculates
  wall deadlines from absolute PAL board cycles at 7,093,790 cycles per second.
  The mouse replay sends (16, 16) every three fields from 700 through 724,
  then (5, 16) at 727, (0, 16) at 730 and 733, and (0, 1) at 736. The
  mouse button is held from field 810 to 816 and from field 1460 to 1466

  Normal priority, excluded its SMT sibling 5, and placed helper threads

  preflight of more than 11 seconds, process execution and a cooldown of
  more than 11 seconds. Both earlier whole-process single-CPU attempts
  were invalidated by detected MSBuild activity.

## Copper68k 1.5.5 package qualification

Copper68k 1.5.5 includes .NET 10 and .NET 11 assets. CopperScreen consumes
the .NET 10 asset. CopperStart.FileSystem.Service 0.5.0-dev.2 carries the
same service assembly and embedded firmware as 0.5.0-dev.1 and declares
Copper68k 1.5.5 as its exact dependency.

Local Release candidate timings used the same frozen ROM, disk, replay
inputs, Windows CPU placement, and PAL deadline calculations described above.
